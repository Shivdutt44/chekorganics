From a farm in Telangana, with purpose.

CHEKORGANICS was not born in a boardroom or a laboratory. It was born in a field — a certified organic Noni farm in Telangana, tended by a woman who walked away from a comfortable life abroad to return to her roots, her soil, and her calling.

Every product you hold in your hands carries the story of that decision. The Ayurvedic wisdom of 5,000 years, grown on our own land, certified by the Government of India, and made with nothing but your well-being in mind.

She had a good life abroad. She chose a better one.
Like many Indians of her generation, she had built a life outside India — a career, a routine, a comfortable distance from village rhythms. But she watched the organic movement grow worldwide while India’s ancient agricultural wisdom was being buried under synthetic chemicals. So she came home — not to retire, but to build.

The fruit that changed everything: Ashyuka (Noni)

When she began exploring what to grow on her land in Telangana, she kept returning to one plant — Noni. Known in Sanskrit as Ashyuka, the Noni fruit has been used in Ayurvedic practice for thousands of years as a herb of longevity. Modern science has identified over 150+ beneficial nutrients, antioxidants, and bioactive compounds within it.

In a market full of brands using Noni as a marketing label, she decided to actually grow it. Organically. Traceably. On her own land, in Telangana's fertile climate, without a single synthetic input.

In 2018, the National Nutrition and Health Association recognized her Noni cultivation with the honour of India's Best Organic Farmer for Noni. Today, that Noni becomes the active ingredient in your hair oil, body butter, herbal shampoo, health elixir, and soaps. There are no middlemen between our field and your hands.

“The land does not belong to the person who holds the deed. It belongs to the community it feeds.”

— Founder, CHEKORGANICS · Nari Shakti Awardee 2025

A Pandemic. A Choice. 550 Families.

In 2020, when India went into lockdown and daily wage families across Telangana faced the prospect of hunger, she did not pause to calculate the cost. She delivered food essentials to 550 families.

Eenadu newspaper covered the story. But the story was not the media coverage — it was the conviction. The same conviction that refuses to put a single synthetic chemical or filler into our products, because the bodies of the people who trust us are not a commercial opportunity — they are our sacred responsibility.
